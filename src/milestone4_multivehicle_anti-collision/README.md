# Milestone 4: four drones with ORCA anti-collision

ROS/Python package: `milestone4_multivehicle`. Extract this folder under
`~/px4_ros_com_ws/src/`; milestone 3 can remain installed separately.

```bash
cd ~/px4_ros_com_ws
source /opt/ros/humble/setup.bash
colcon build --symlink-install --packages-up-to milestone4_multivehicle
source install/setup.bash
colcon test --packages-select milestone4_multivehicle
colcon test-result --verbose
```

The package has two launch files: `collision_launch.py` flies four head-on
routes that meet at one point, and `scan_launch.py` scans the competition field
(see below). Both share `milestone4_multivehicle/fleet_launch.py`. Each flies one
vehicle and starts no simulator, so run it once per vehicle: `system_id` N is
instance N-1. Each picks its own route and origin from the same arguments.

The easiest way is the web launcher in SITL mode. On its Fleet tab pick the
**20 m square** spawn layout before adding the four SITL drones (the default line
layout won't make the routes converge), then pick this file on the Mission tab,
tick all four and press Start. With SITL (or real drones) already running, by hand,
each in its own sourced terminal:

```bash
ros2 launch milestone4_multivehicle collision_launch.py system_id:=1
ros2 launch milestone4_multivehicle collision_launch.py topic_prefix:=/px4_1 system_id:=2
ros2 launch milestone4_multivehicle collision_launch.py topic_prefix:=/px4_2 system_id:=3
ros2 launch milestone4_multivehicle collision_launch.py topic_prefix:=/px4_3 system_id:=4
```

Defaults put instances 0–3 at world XY `(0,0)`, `(20,0)`, `(20,20)`, `(0,20)`.
The diagonal routes converge at `(10,10)` at 5 m altitude, then continue to the
opposite corners. Equal holds do not synchronize departure; this is a convergence
stress test, not a guaranteed simultaneous or collision-free encounter.

`spawn_x_1/y_1` through `spawn_x_4/y_4` set the fixed controller origins and
`path_1` through `path_4` the routes; pass the same values to every drone.
World X=East, Y=North; YAML positions are local `[North, East, Down]`.
`vehicle_count:=2` or `3` flies a smaller fleet; with `vehicle_count:=2` and
`path_1`/`path_2` set to `milestone4_collision_0.yaml`/`_1.yaml` it flies the
two-drone offset pass.

### Field scan: `scan_launch.py`

A scan of the 80 by 300 ft competition field (24.384 by 91.44 m) at 5 m, with the
camera footprint taken as 2 m across the direction of travel by 3 m along it, so
rows are 2 m apart and each row ends 1.5 m inside the field:

| Drone | Route | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 2, 3 | Its third of the width, north-south rows over the whole length, starting at the south end; then home to its spawn and land | 4, 4 and 5 (13 in all: 12 at 2 m spacing plus one flush with the east edge) |
| 4 | East-west rows over the whole field, starting at the south edge and working north, crossing the other three; lands at the north end | 46 |

Drones face along each row, so they yaw 180 degrees at every turn. On the web
launcher pick the **South edge of the field** layout before adding the four SITL
drones: they wait in a row 3 m south of the field at 1, 9, 17 and 23 m east of its
south-west corner. "Place a test field around SITL 1" on the Map then puts the
field there. The routes are `resource/milestone4_scan_N.yaml`, generated by
`milestone4_multivehicle/scan_routes.py`; change that and run
`python3 -m milestone4_multivehicle.scan_routes resource/` rather than editing
the YAMLs (a test checks they match). Drone 4 flies about 1.1 km, roughly 18
minutes at 1 m/s; the others finish in 6 to 9 minutes.

### Anti-collision: `avoidance:=orca` (default) or `avoidance:=priority`

**ORCA** (Optimal Reciprocal Collision Avoidance, `orca.py` and
`orca_avoidance.py`). Every drone shares `[instance, global_x, global_y,
global_vx, global_vy]` (world East/North) and every drone avoids; there is no
priority. Each 10 Hz tick a drone takes the velocity along the leg from its
previous waypoint to this one, aiming 2 m ahead of the closest point on the leg so
it steers back onto the line after a sidestep (up to `max_speed`, slowing within
about 1.25 m of the waypoint), and ORCA returns the
closest velocity that keeps it at least 2 x 1.5 m from every peer for the next
3 s, assuming each peer does half the work. PX4 flies that as a **velocity
setpoint** in x/y (position x/y are NaN) with the waypoint's altitude as a
position setpoint in z, so drones sidestep at their own height and never stop
to climb. Waypoint arrival and holds keep counting while avoiding; the final
landing waits until ORCA has been quiet for 1 s. Details:

- Exactly head-on pairs would both slow to a stop, so while ORCA is changing a
  drone's velocity it turns its preferred velocity 10 degrees right. (Until run 5
  this applied whenever a peer was within 10 m, which bowed scan rows by up to
  4 m next to a neighbouring strip.) If a drone is
  still held below 0.2 m/s for 1.5 s, it turns 70 degrees right for 4 s, so a
  stuck group circles the same way like a roundabout.
- A peer silent for 0.5 s stays an obstacle at its last position, with a
  2.25 m radius, and this drone avoids it alone. Every peer must be heard from
  before arming.
- If its own velocity is invalid the drone holds position.
- `max_speed` (default 1.0 m/s) should match the drones' `MPC_XY_VEL_MAX`.
  Give every drone the same `avoidance` and `max_speed`; a drone ignores
  messages from the other policy, so a mixed fleet will not arm.

**Priority** (the original milestone 3 rule, `avoidance.py`). Priority is
**3 > 2 > 1 > 0**. At horizontal distance <=5 m from any higher instance, the
lower instance brakes, climbs once by 5 m from its encounter altitude, and holds
until **all** higher instances are outside the radius and have fresh messages.
The interrupted waypoint resumes with its hold reset. The shared payload is
`[instance, global_x, global_y]`, and setpoints are positions.

The controller sets no PX4 parameters. The web launcher's SITL drones use a
1 m/s horizontal cap, 1 m/s climb/descent and 45 degree/s yaw. The cap was 2 m/s
until 2026-11-12; at 2 m/s the first SITL run failed with a mid-air collision.
See the results log.

On the head-on test at 1 m/s, priority passed drones 2.12 m apart after a 5 m
climb (run 2), and ORCA kept every pair at least 2.99 m apart at 5 m (run 3).
The priority rule is reactive and ignores Z: yielding drones may climb to the
same altitude, and a stationary higher-priority drone can block a lower drone
indefinitely. ORCA is also XY-only: drones at different heights, or one landed,
still avoid each other horizontally.
Use SITL for milestone 4. See [testing and limitations](documentation/milestone-4-four-vehicle.md).
